The problem with practising this on your own.

Eleven fingerings, three ways through the strings, five positions, two starting strokes. It is a lot to hold in your head, and holding it in your head is exactly what stops you paying attention to how you sound.

So the app holds it for you. It runs all 33 exercises as one unbroken pass, in time, with the tab moving under a playhead, and it keeps a record of which positions and which starting strokes you have actually covered.

What the routine covers.

  • Every fingering. Six two-finger combinations, four three-finger, and all four together. Including the awkward ones most players quietly avoid.
  • Three ways through the strings. Arpeggiated, adjacent strings and string skipping, for every fingering.
  • Five positions. The neck feels different everywhere. Being comfortable at the first fret says nothing about the thirteenth.
  • Both starting strokes. Start on an upstroke and the whole thing turns around. The app flips it with one tap and tracks which way you did it.
  • One unbroken pass. Strict alternate picking straight through, no stopping between exercises.
  • Tab written as fingers, not frets. Move the routine to the fifth position and the tab still reads 1 and 2, so you always know which finger combination you are on instead of doing sums in your head.

Slow, on purpose.

These exercises sound like nothing. That is the most useful thing about them.

When you practise a lick you have heard at full speed, you push toward that speed without deciding to. There is nothing to chase here, so you are free to play it as slowly as it takes to play it properly, which is the only thing that makes practice work.

Start at 50 BPM. I do it at 60 to 80. The whole point is getting everything perfect, and that is what gives you technique you can actually use in music.
