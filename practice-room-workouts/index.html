The hard part of technique practice is not the playing.

It is everything around it. Which exercise, which position, which key, which starting stroke, how long, and did I already do this one on Tuesday. That is the stuff that stops you before you have played a note.

So the app holds it for you. Choose a workout, press start, and the tab moves under a playhead in time with the metronome. It runs the whole thing in order, flips the picking direction when it is time, moves you through the positions, and writes down what you covered. Your only job is to play it cleanly.

What the app does while you play.

  • Tab under a playhead. The notes light up as they come. You never lose your place, and you never have to look away from the screen to check what is next.
  • A metronome that knows the routine. Count-in, subdivisions, and the tempo you set. Change it between passes without stopping to think about it.
  • Hear it before you play it. Every workout plays back on a clean electric, a nylon string, a grand piano or a harpsichord, at whatever tempo you are at. Turn it down or off once you have it.
  • Focus on one sequence. Run the full workout, or pick the one sequence that is giving you trouble and take it through every position on its own. The app ticks it off when you have done it in every position in that key.
  • Rate what you played. After a pass, mark the patterns as needs work, getting there, or comfortable, at the tempo you played them. Next session, you know exactly where to start.
  • Every key, every position, in order. Twelve keys, five positions, both starting strokes, depending on the workout. The app moves you through them so you cover the neck instead of the two spots you like.
  • A log that fills itself in. Every session goes into your calendar with the workout, the tempo, the key or position and the time. The coverage view shows which positions and starting strokes you have actually done, so you stop repeating the comfortable one.

Slow, on purpose.

These exercises sound like nothing. That is the most useful thing about them.

When you practise a lick you have heard at full speed, you push toward that speed without deciding to. There is nothing to chase here, so you are free to play it as slowly as it takes to play it properly, which is the only thing that makes practice work.

Start at 50 BPM. I do most of this at 60 to 80. The whole point is getting everything perfect, and that is what gives you technique you can actually use in music.

I have been teaching this way since 2001, and I studied it at GIT before that. The app is the version of it I would have wanted at 20: it does the admin so I can do the playing.
