I hear from guitarists every week who are overwhelmed. Too many methods, too many opinions, no idea what to work on today. And in most cases the root cause is the same: they will not stop tinkering.

You're changing exercises. Changing routines. Switching your picking motion because a video told you your wrist is wrong. Dropping alternate picking after three weeks to chase legato. Running a metronome program one week, then "just playing musically" the next because someone said metronomes kill your feel.

You're constantly making adjustments because you watched another video or heard someone say something different. And respectfully, you haven't built the technique yet to know whether those adjustments even make sense.

I say that with love, because I did exactly this for years. Every time I read a new interview I rebuilt my picking hand from scratch. Petrucci one month, Yngwie the next. I've written before about [what that cost me](/notes/alternate-picking-mistakes-that-cost-me-years/). The short version: years.

## The one source rule

Here's the rule that fixes it: pick one teacher, or one philosophy, that makes sense to you, and block everything else out.

I mean that literally. If I'm your one source for this stuff, back it up. When another guitar channel pops up promising a better picking motion, a smarter routine, some new hack you need to try, click "do not recommend channel." And if your one source is someone else, that's fine too. Pick them properly and do the same to me.

Better yet, get off guitar social media as a habit altogether. YouTube, Instagram, all of it. Stop filling your brain with noise between practice sessions.

I know that sounds strange coming from someone whose job is making guitar videos. But I'm not making them because I want you sitting there watching me forever. I want you to learn what you need, close the app, and go put it into your hands.

## Pick a plan and follow it

I've given away a lot on this site and on the channel. Free exercises. Practice sessions you can press play on and follow along with. Videos on picking, legato, timing, speed.

Pick a plan and follow it.

And if you want everything laid out step by step, in one system you own, that's what [The Practice Room](https://www.jonbjorkmusic.com/practiceroom) is for.

Either way, give me 365 days. Follow the plan and let time do its job.

## The fastest path is the slowest one

If what you're currently doing isn't working, take this seriously. Tinkering is one of the biggest things holding guitarists back today. And you're tinkering because you're consuming so much information that you've convinced yourself there has to be a faster way.

There isn't. The fastest path to the playing you want is usually the one that looks slowest from the outside. Pick a direction, stop changing it, and give it time.
