Your Path.

Twenty courses and eight Labs is a lot to walk into. This is the order I'd put them in. Pick one line, work it to the end, then come back and pick the next one.

Before You Start

One Track At A Time.

The fastest way to get nothing out of this room is to open five courses in the first week and do a bit of each. Everything in here is built to be finished, and finishing one thing beats sampling six.

So pick one technique to start with. If you can't decide, pick the one that's the reason you skip certain songs.

The Shape Of It

Every Track Runs In Three Phases.

Same shape whichever technique you choose. Roughly four months from the first day of a Deep Dive to the end of an Intensive.

Phase 1

The Deep Dive

30 minutes a day for 30 days. It builds correct practice habits, fixes the problems you already have with the technique, and if it's new to you, it teaches you to play it correctly from the beginning. Do this before the Intensive.

Phase 2

The Intensive

Eight weeks, a new 60-minute routine every week. You're never repeating the same hour twice. The routine is already built, so your only job is to show up and run it.

Phase 3

Keep It Alive

The application material. Speed Systems, Chops Builders and the daily workouts take the technique you just built and put it to work while you move on to the next track.

Underneath all three phases sits the Practice Lab. The Lab doesn't replace a course. It's where you take the one thing in this week's routine that keeps falling apart. There's a full map of which Lab to open further down.

The Three Main Tracks.

Alternate picking, legato and sweep picking each have a Deep Dive and an Intensive behind them. These are the long roads.

30 Days Each

The Standalone Deep Dives.

These don't have an intensive behind them. Run one between tracks, or alongside a track if you've got the time.

Timing Deep Dive

Subdivisions, counting and precision. This is the one I'd run alongside another track, because timing makes every other technique sound better. Pair it with the Rhythm Lab.

Hybrid Picking Deep Dive

The mechanics of hybrid picking and the essential patterns. Easiest to add once your alternate picking is already solid.

Tapping Deep Dive

The mechanics of tapping and the essential patterns, applied to scales and arpeggios. Shorter than the other Deep Dives, so it fits into a gap.

Quick Licks

Not a Deep Dive, but this is where the exercises pay off. What you build in a track is scaffolding, and speed shows up on real musical lines where the phrase sets the tempo. Worth running alongside a track rather than saving for the end.

Runs Alongside

The Musicianship Track.

This one doesn't wait its turn. Your ear and your fretboard knowledge can develop at the same time as your technique, and they should.

Playing What You Hear

Start here for the ear, before you open the Ear Lab. It's an organized program for developing your ear and your fretboard knowledge together. The biggest gains come from singing the solfeggio syllables out loud, so do that part properly even when it feels silly.

Nailing The Minor Pentatonic

Twelve weeks, one key a week, all the way around the fretboard. Single strings, string groups, string skipping, every picking style, legato, tapping, bends and slides, with fretboard triangulation and solfege running through it.

The Ear Lab is a companion to Playing What You Hear, not the way in. Work the course, then use the Lab to keep the skill sharp between sessions.
Ten Minutes Is Enough

When You Don't Have An Hour.

Some weeks the routine isn't going to happen. That's no reason to do nothing, and no reason to wait until life calms down before you start.

The Daily Ten

Pick a technique and play along with me for ten minutes. 150 workouts, and no two routines need to look alike.

10-Minute Workouts Vol. 1 & 2

28 workouts, each one covering a scale or a tonality completely inside a single ten-minute session.

Guitar Gym

Around 36 targeted workouts in three groups: warm-ups, mechanical practice, and scales and arpeggios. Rotate between the three, and stay on one exercise per session until you own it.

On a day with twenty minutes and no plan at all, open Quick Session in the Practice Lab. Pick for me, choose a technique, or build your own.

Twenty minutes that actually happens beats an hour you keep postponing.

Getting into the Lab: the link and the current password are posted in the Classroom. It's a shared password for everyone in the room and it rotates every few weeks, so if it stops working, go and grab the new one. Nothing has expired and you haven't lost anything.

One more thing. If the Virtuoso Lab tells you you're better off pausing, take it seriously. It only says that when your accurate reps have stopped holding together, and the reps you've already banked consolidate overnight. Stopping there is the productive move.

Day To Day

What This Looks Like In A Week.

  • The routine from your current course is the practice session. Almost every program in here has its routine built in, so you're not designing anything. Open it and run it.
  • Ten extra minutes in a Lab on the thing that broke. There's always one bar, one shape or one string crossing that falls apart. That's the Lab's job, not the routine's.
  • Log it. The streak and the heatmap on the Dashboard are how you find out whether you practiced this week or only meant to.
  • Post the sticking point in the room. The whole reason the community is there is so you're not guessing on your own between sessions.
One Last Thing

Finishing Isn't The Point.

The goal isn't to complete as many courses as possible. The goal is to become a better guitar player. If one Deep Dive and one Intensive is all you get through this year, and your picking comes out the other side clean and fast, that's the room doing its job.
