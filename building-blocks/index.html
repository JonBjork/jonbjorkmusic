The Building
Blocks Workout

Three notes at a time. Every finger combination, every string crossing, both directions. The smallest unit of technique, trained on purpose.

This is not a lick. It's the alphabet.

Long passages are these three-note shapes stacked. If you only ever drill the long passage, every new one is a new thing. Drill the alphabet and the passages get easy.

4 finger combinations · 16 string crossings · Picking or legato

Why this works

Three notes is the right unit.

01

Russell's principle

Classical guitarist David Russell teaches that the smallest building blocks deserve their own practice time. Every long passage is these tiny units stacked. Train the units. The passages take care of themselves.

02

Cover every crossing

Three notes inside a string feels nothing like three notes that cross strings. Two-plus-one and one-plus-two each have their own picking and timing problem. You have to play all of them on purpose, not just the comfortable ones.

03

One workout, two techniques

The same patterns serve alternate picking and legato. Picking trains the right hand's response to each crossing. Legato trains your timing without the pick covering for you. Same shapes, two different muscles.
