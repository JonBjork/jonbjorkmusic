01
Stage 2  ·  Program

Planting & Rhythmic Planting

Set the pick before you play the note. Play on the downbeat, plant and pre-finger on the upbeat.

Use it when

Your picking motion is bigger than it needs to be, string changes feel uncontrolled, or you are learning a phrase from scratch and want the first impression to be a clean one.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. Open G string, alternate strokes at a slow pulse. Plant the pick immediately after each note. The shorter and more even you can make the notes, the better you are doing.
  2. 9-10-11-12 on the G string. Keep the pick close enough that you can barely see it leave the string, and let it come to rest against the other side after every stroke.
  3. The same pattern with rhythmic planting: play on each downbeat, plant and pre-finger both hands on each upbeat.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Play your phrase slowly with rhythmic planting. Play on the downbeat, prepare both hands on the upbeat.
  2. Take one string change out of it. As you cross to the new string, mute the one you came from by releasing the pressure in the fretting hand. You want the sound to stay uniform across the crossing.
  3. If the picking is the problem, mute the strings and play the picking pattern alone with plain planting.

You've got it when

The notes come out short and even, and a string change no longer stands out from the notes on either side of it.

02
Stage 2  ·  Program

Transition Time

Slow tempo, quick transitions. Hold each note for its full value, then move quickly to the next.

Use it when

Your slow practice uses slow-motion movements that look nothing like the technique you need at speed, or your tone is uneven and mushy at every tempo.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. 9-10-11-12 on the G string at 60, one note per beat. Hold each note for its full value, then move quickly to the next.
  2. The same pattern, making the fretting change quick without lifting the fingers high.
  3. The same again, making the pick pass through the string quickly without adding any force.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Set quarter notes somewhere between 50 and 80. Hold each note of the phrase, then move decisively at the last instant.
  2. Apply it to one string crossing or position shift on its own.
  3. Play the whole phrase slowly, keeping the same decisive transitions all the way through.

You've got it when

Every note lasts right up to the next one with no gap, while the movement between them feels sharper and faster than the tempo does.

03
Stage 3  ·  Stress-test

Dotted Rhythms

Long-short through the phrase, then short-long. Covers every transition at speed.

Use it when

The phrase collapses as you approach the tempo you want, and you need to feel the transitions at speed without playing the whole thing at speed.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. 9-10-11-12 on the G string. Long, short, long, short. Hold the first note as long as you can, then snap into the second.
  2. The same pattern reversed: short, long, short, long.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Long-short through the phrase at a comfortable tempo.
  2. Reverse it to short-long.
  3. Find the fastest tempo where both directions still pass RAT, and stay there.
  4. Put the dotted figure inside your target subdivision, at the target tempo.
  5. Play it straight and notice which transitions now hold.

You've got it when

You can play both directions cleanly at the subdivision you are aiming for, and playing the phrase straight feels like joining up transitions you have already made.

04
Stage 3  ·  Stress-test

Accents

Accent note 1 of each beat, then 2, then 3, then 4. Both hands accent together.

Use it when

The two hands do not feel joined, your timing is uneven, or there are notes in the middle of a phrase you suspect are sloppy without being able to hear it.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. A G major scale in sixteenths. Accent note 1 of every beat, counting out loud.
  2. The same scale accenting note 2, then note 3, then note 4.
  3. Feel the accent arrive in both hands at the same instant.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Accent note 1 of each beat through your phrase.
  2. Then note 2, then 3, then 4. Triplets go 1, 2, 3.
  3. Stay on whichever position turned out to be hardest.
  4. For a fragment only a few notes long, accent each note of the fragment in turn instead.

You've got it when

You can place the accent on any note of the beat without the beat itself moving, and both hands arrive on it together.

05
Stage 5  ·  Make it music

Dynamics

Soft to loud and back. Stay relaxed and in control at every dynamic level. Ride a wave up and down the phrase.

Use it when

The phrase is technically correct and sounds mechanical, or you have been repeating something long enough that you have stopped listening to it.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. One note on a clean sound, as softly as you can while the tone stays even.
  2. The same note at a normal level, then as loud as still sounds musical.
  3. A scale, soft going up and loud coming down. Then reverse it.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Play your phrase as softly as you can with an even tone.
  2. Bring it up to a normal level, then as loud as still sounds musical, staying relaxed throughout.
  3. Shape it: soft at the start, swelling, back down. Then improvise the shape freely.

You've got it when

You can play the same phrase at three clearly different volumes with the same tone and the same relaxation in each.

06
Stage 3  ·  Stress-test

Looping

Number the notes and start the loop on each one in turn, so you can begin anywhere. Start on the correct pick stroke.

Use it when

One repeating cell is unreliable, or you want a lot of good repetitions on a problem spot without your attention going flat.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Number the notes of the loop.
  2. Play variation 1 as written, with a slight accent on the starting note.
  3. Start on note 2, go round the loop, and come back to note 2. Check the starting stroke from the top.
  4. Continue through every note of the loop in turn.
  5. Stay on whichever variation was hardest.

You've got it when

Every variation flows without stumbling, and you can start the loop on any note without having to think about it.

07
Stage 4  ·  Accelerate

Speed Bursts

Notes 1-2. Then 1-2-3. Then 1-2-3-4. Plant, relax, play. Then work backwards from the end.

Use it when

You have already worked the phrase with the slower tools, you can play it accurately at a moderate tempo, and you want to cross a speed barrier.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. Take 9-10-11-12 on the G string, numbered 1 to 4, and play notes 1 and 2 only. Plant, relax, play, and pause properly between every repetition.
  2. Add note 3 only once you have played 1 and 2 perfectly five times in a row. Then note 4 the same way.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Number the notes. Play notes 1 and 2 as fast as you can while still passing RAT, pausing between repetitions.
  2. Five perfect repetitions in a row before you add note 3, then note 4, and on to the end. Start a new burst wherever it falls apart.
  3. Then work from the end: the last two notes, then the last three.
  4. If a burst goes sloppy, drop back a note and apply Transition Time or Accents there.

You've got it when

The full-length burst runs faster than your clean tempo for the whole phrase, and you are still relaxed at the end of it.

08
Stage 1  ·  Diagnose

Beat to Beat

X+1 notes, beat to beat, at 80% of target. Start on each note of the phrase in turn. This is what finds the weak links.

Use it when

You do not know where the glitch actually is, or you have a phrase down at a slow tempo and want to know what is stopping it going faster.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Set the metronome to about 80% of your target tempo.
  2. Play the first X+1 group: the notes of one beat, plus the first note of the next.
  3. Start the group on note 2 instead. Then note 3. Then note 4.
  4. Continue to the end of the phrase, checking the starting pick stroke each time.
  5. Write down the two or three groups that failed. Those are your weak links.

You've got it when

Every X+1 group plays at 80% of the target with RAT intact, and you can name the two or three that do not.

09
Stage 3  ·  Stress-test

Rhythmic Variation

Play the phrase with 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 notes per beat. The accents move as you change.

Use it when

The groupings feel locked in, your timing is unreliable, or you want to make a lick you learned from someone else genuinely your own.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. 9-10-11-12 on the G string in sixteenths, counting out loud.
  2. The same four notes in triplets. Notice the accent moving through them and coming back round.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Play the phrase in its written subdivision.
  2. Switch to the opposing one: triplets to sixteenths, or the reverse.
  3. Count out loud all the way through.
  4. Try one unfamiliar subdivision, such as fives or sevens.
  5. Come back to the written one.

You've got it when

You can change subdivision without stopping, and the accent moving through the phrase no longer throws you.

10
Anywhere  ·  No guitar needed

Visualization

Away from the guitar: hear it, see the fretboard, feel the stroke. Three or four notes to start.

Use it when

You cannot clearly hear or remember the phrase, you are learning something new from notation, or you are away from the guitar with time to spare.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. Away from the guitar, imagine one note: the finger on the fret, the pick planted, then the sound.
  2. Add a second note, then combine the two.
  3. Build to four notes and run them at a steady slow tempo.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Take the first few notes of your phrase and build them the same way.
  2. Keep adding until you can run the whole phrase in your head at a slow, steady tempo.
  3. Then apply a tool mentally: move an accent, or imagine a dotted rhythm.

You've got it when

You can run the phrase in your head from start to finish at a steady slow tempo, hearing and seeing each note as it goes past.

11
Stage 3  ·  Stress-test

Practice on an Acoustic

Move the problem spot to acoustic. Accuracy rather than force. Keep the tempo slow.

Use it when

You suspect gain and compression are covering for you, or your legato is nearly there and you cannot tell what is missing.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Move the passage that is giving you trouble to a steel-string or a nylon.
  2. Play it slowly, using the minimum force that sounds each note cleanly.
  3. Work the hardest weak link on its own.
  4. Practise Dynamics on it, which has more range here than anywhere else.
  5. Go back to the electric at the tempo you had before.

You've got it when

The passage sounds clean on the acoustic at a tempo you can hold, without using any more force than you would on the electric.

12
Stage 1  ·  Diagnose

Hands Separate

Picking hand on muted strings. Fretting hand, all hammer-ons unless the phrase has real legato. Find the hand holding you back.

Use it when

A weak link fails and you cannot tell where the fault is, or you want to work one hand without the other's problems in the way.

Get the feel for it

Once, on a neutral pattern, to learn what the tool feels like.

  1. Mute the strings with the fretting hand so nothing sounds, and play a simple alternate-picked pattern. Listen only to the rhythm of the pick.
  2. Damp the strings with the picking hand and sound 9-10-11-12 with hammer-ons alone. Listen only to the rhythm of the fingers.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Narrow the problem down to three to six notes first.
  2. Picking hand alone: mute the strings and play the exact picking pattern, same strokes and string changes.
  3. Fretting hand alone: mute the strings with a cloth or hair band and play the fingering with hammer-ons in both directions, unless the phrase has real pull-offs in it.
  4. Put the two hands back together, slowly.

You've got it when

Each hand plays its part cleanly on its own, and you know for sure which one was causing the problem.

13
Stage 1  ·  Diagnose & verify

Record Yourself

Thirty seconds on your phone. Watch it back. Fix. Record again.

Use it when

You want an objective listener, or you want to know whether a specific practice tool did its job.

On your phrase

Every session, on your own music.

  1. Record thirty seconds of the phrase.
  2. Watch it back straight away and note three things.
  3. Fix one of them.
  4. Record again and compare the two takes.

You've got it when

What you hear on playback matches what you thought you were playing while you played it.
